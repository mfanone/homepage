Michigan's permit system is simpler than most states, but the terminology trips people up. Here's what you actually need, and when.

## Recreation Passport

This is Michigan's state park vehicle entry pass — required for any vehicle entering a state park, state recreation area, DNR boat launch, or state forest campground.

- **Resident cost:** $15/year when purchased at license plate registration renewal ($29 for a two-year plate, $7 for motorcycles)
- **Nonresident cost:** around $41/year, or roughly $11/day
- **Buying it later** (at a park gate or DNR office instead of at renewal) adds a $5 convenience fee

It's tied to your vehicle, not your person — two cars means two passports, or carpool.

**Do you need it for state forest dispersed camping?** Generally no, if you're not driving into a state park or designated DNR access site to get there. But many trailheads for popular routes (Porcupine Mountains, some North Country Trail access points) do sit inside state park or recreation area boundaries, so check your specific trailhead.

## Backcountry camping permits

Separate from the Recreation Passport. Required for overnight backcountry camping inside a state park (Porcupine Mountains backcountry, for example). Typically booked in advance through the DNR reservation system, with a per-night fee that varies by park.

State forest dispersed camping does **not** require this kind of permit — that's the core distinction between "state forest land" and "state park backcountry."

## National Park entrance fees (Isle Royale)

Different system entirely, run by the National Park Service, not Michigan DNR. Isle Royale charges a per-person daily entrance fee, with a season pass option if you're visiting more than once. See the full breakdown in the [Isle Royale budget guide](/guides/isle-royale-on-a-budget/).

## Quick reference

| Where you're going | What you need |
|---|---|
| State forest dispersed camping | Usually nothing |
| State park trailhead / day hike | Recreation Passport |
| State park backcountry overnight | Recreation Passport + backcountry camping permit |
| National Forest dispersed camping | Usually nothing |
| Isle Royale | NPS entrance fee + free backcountry camping permit (issued on arrival) |

When in doubt, check the specific trail or park's DNR or NPS page before you go — rules and exact fees are periodically updated, and this guide reflects the general structure rather than guaranteed current pricing.

---
title: "Isle Royale on a budget"
date: 2026-07-22
---

Isle Royale is Michigan's most expensive backpacking destination by far — almost entirely because of ferry costs, not the park itself. Here's where the money actually goes, and where you can cut it.

## The real cost driver: getting there

Isle Royale is only reachable by ferry or seaplane, and this is where nearly your entire budget goes. The NPS-run Ranger III ferry from Houghton has historically run in the $70-80 range per adult, one-way — meaning round-trip ferry transportation alone can easily be your single biggest trip expense, well above gear, food, or park fees combined. Private ferry operators from Copper Harbor and Grand Portage (Minnesota) offer alternative routes and pricing, worth comparing directly since costs and schedules shift year to year.

**Budget tip:** compare all departure points before booking. Grand Portage is a longer drive for most Michiganders but sometimes prices out cheaper overall once you factor in the shorter crossing.

## Park entrance fee

Isle Royale charges a per-person daily entrance fee (around $7/person/day as of recent seasons), with a season pass option (around $60) if you're spreading your visit across return trips, or if the per-day math for your trip length exceeds the season pass price. The America the Beautiful annual pass also covers this entrance fee if you already hold one for other park visits.

## Backcountry camping: actually free

Unlike the ferry and entrance fee, backcountry camping permits at Isle Royale are free and issued on arrival — no advance reservation needed for individual/small group camping. This is the one part of an Isle Royale trip that doesn't cost extra.

## A realistic budget for a 4-day trip

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Ferry (round trip) | $140-160 |
| Park entrance fee (4 days) | $28 |
| Backcountry camping | Free |
| Food | $40-50 |
| **Total** | **~$210-240** |

That's a meaningfully bigger number than a Lower Peninsula trip, and it's almost entirely the ferry. If Isle Royale is on your list but the ferry cost is the blocker, a shorter 2-3 day trip cuts your food cost without touching the fixed ferry price — meaning the per-day cost of an Isle Royale trip actually drops the longer you stay, once you've already paid for the crossing.

## Where to cut, realistically

- **Go with a group and split gear weight/shared meals** — doesn't reduce ferry cost (that's per-person), but meaningfully cuts food and fuel costs
- **Buy the season pass only if you're confident you'll return** within the same season — otherwise the daily rate is usually cheaper for a single trip
- **Book the ferry early** — prices and availability tighten up significantly during peak summer season

Isle Royale isn't going to hit the same $20/day target as a Lower Peninsula trip, and that's fine — it's a fundamentally different kind of trip. Budget for it separately rather than trying to force it into the same per-day math as everything else on this site.

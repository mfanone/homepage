Michigan is one of the cheapest places in the country to backpack, if you know where the costs actually come from. Here's a realistic day-rate budget, broken down by category.

## The big three costs

**Gas.** For most Lower Peninsula trailheads, you're looking at a 2-4 hour drive from Southeast Michigan. Split between a weekend trip, that's often $10-20 total — cheap if you're not driving solo.

**Permits and passes.** A [Recreation Passport](/guides/permits-and-passes/) covers state park and trailhead vehicle access for $15/year if bought with your plate renewal (about $41/year if you're a nonresident). DNR backcountry campsite fees vary by trail, but many are $0-15/night.

**Food.** Backpacking food doesn't have to mean expensive dehydrated meal pouches. A grocery-store base of instant oats, tortillas, peanut butter, ramen, and trail mix runs $8-12/day per person, easily.

## A realistic $20/day breakdown

| Category | Cost |
|---|---|
| Gas (split across trip) | $5-8/day |
| Permit/campsite fee | $0-5/day |
| Food | $8-12/day |
| **Total** | **~$18-20/day** |

This assumes you already own your core gear (pack, shelter, sleep system) — those are one-time costs, not daily ones, and they're the real budget-breaker if you're starting from zero. If you're building a kit from scratch, budget separately and don't try to squeeze gear purchases into a per-day trip number.

## Where the savings actually come from

- **Michigan's state forest land allows free dispersed camping** in most areas outside designated state park campgrounds — see the [free camping guide](/guides/free-cheap-camping-michigan/) for specifics
- **Buying your Recreation Passport at license renewal** instead of at the gate saves the $5 convenience fee
- **Cooking your own food** instead of buying backpacker meal pouches is easily half the cost for the same calories
- **Carpooling to trailheads** splits your single biggest variable cost

None of this requires ultralight gear or specialized budget hacks — it's mostly about knowing Michigan's specific fee structure, which is genuinely cheaper than most Western states once you know where to look.

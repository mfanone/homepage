Manistee River Trail Loop is one of the best beginner-friendly overnights in the Lower Peninsula, and it barely dents your wallet. Here's how the trip went, and exactly what it cost.

## The route

The loop follows the Manistee River through old-growth pine, crossing two suspension bridges and passing three backcountry campsites spaced roughly 8 miles apart. Elevation change is moderate and the trail underfoot is soft, so it's a forgiving first overnight if you're newer to multi-day hiking. Water access from the river is reliable the whole way, which kept my pack weight down since I never carried more than a liter at a time.

Day one covered the first 11 miles to the second campsite, with a lunch break at the suspension bridge overlook. Day two finished the loop back to the trailhead, a shorter 13-mile push but with a few more rolling climbs near the end.

No gear purchases this trip — everything I used was already in my kit. If you're starting from zero, budget separately for a pack, shelter, and sleep system; those are one-time costs that pay off over many trips.

## Would I do it again?

Yes, easily. This is the loop I'd point a first-time backpacker toward if they wanted to try Michigan's Lower Peninsula without committing to something bigger like Isle Royale or the North Country Trail. Bring a water filter or purification tablets, pack layers for cool nights near the river, and you're set.
